Plato's Cave

From Plato's Republic (c. 375 BCE). Socrates uses two connected images, the sun and the cave, to describe the philosopher's education and the world the philosopher comes to know.

View textual sources

1.  The Sun

Republic VI, 506d–509c

Asked to define the Good, Socrates declines and offers only an analogy. Sight requires three things: a healthy eye, something to see, and a third element – light – whose source is the sun. The Good plays the same role in the intelligible world: it is the source from which truth flows, and truth is what makes it possible for the mind to know the Forms. 2.  The Cave

Republic VII, 514a–517a

Prisoners chained since childhood face the back wall of a cave. Behind them a fire burns; between fire and prisoners runs a low parapet along which puppeteers carry figures of men, animals and artefacts. The prisoners see only the shadows the figures throw on the wall and take these for reality. The story that follows traces what happens when one prisoner is released.
