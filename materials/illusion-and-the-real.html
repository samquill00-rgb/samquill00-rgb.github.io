Illusion and the Real

1.  The Checker-Shadow Illusion

1995  ·  Computer-generated image  ·  Edward H. Adelson, Massachusetts Institute of Technology

The image shows a checkerboard with a green cylinder casting a shadow across it. The squares marked A and B are printed in exactly the same grey, yet B looks markedly lighter, because the visual system reads it as a pale square lying in shadow and adjusts accordingly.

“As with many so-called illusions, this effect really demonstrates the success rather than the failure of the visual system.”

– Edward H. Adelson

2.  The Perspective Gallery, Palazzo Spada

1653  ·  Forced-perspective colonnade by Francesco Borromini  ·  Palazzo Spada, Rome

Borromini built a colonnade about eight metres long that appears roughly four times that distance. The floor rises, the ceiling lowers and the columns shrink as they recede, so that a statue at the far end, seemingly life-size, in fact stands well under a metre tall.

3.  Dazzle Camouflage

From 1917  ·  Painted ship camouflage  ·  Royal Navy and US Navy, First World War

Dazzle camouflage, devised by the marine artist Norman Wilkinson, covered First World War warships in bold, clashing geometric patterns. It made no attempt to conceal a ship, which is impossible at sea, but worked to break up its outline and confuse an enemy’s estimate of its size, speed and heading.

4.  Georges Méliès: an Early Trick Film

1896–1902  ·  Silent trick films  ·  Georges Méliès, France

Méliès, a stage magician turned film-maker, found that stopping the camera and restarting it let objects and people appear, vanish or transform between one frame and the next. The Vanishing Lady (1896) shows the trick at its simplest, while A Trip to the Moon (1902) assembles a whole fantastical voyage from such effects.

5.  ‘The Commissar Vanishes’

April 1937  ·  Press photograph, before and after retouching  ·  Soviet Union

Under Stalin, officials who fell from favour were removed not only from their posts but from photographs, painted or scraped out until the record appeared never to have included them. Here Nikolai Yezhov, once head of the secret police, walks beside Stalin by the canal in the first photograph, and has been erased from the second after he himself fell from power.
